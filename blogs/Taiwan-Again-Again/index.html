Taiwan Again Again

So I flew to Taiwan one more time. Actually, this is technically "Taiwan Again Again Again". Six months ago I flew with Jacey to hike Hehuanshan, but I didn't post about that.

The reason we flew to Taiwan this time was to climb Mount Yushan, the highest mountain in East Asia! But before we did that, we made sure to stop at Kenting and Alishan before continuing on to Yushan. Also, this time it was me, Jacey, and Hagan.

Kenting

We landed in Taipei and immediately made our grueling journey down to the south of Taiwan. You might ask yourself, "Tom, why didn't you land at Kaohsiung Airport instead of landing in Taipei and taking the HSR?" Well, the answer is that we are stupid.

Anyway, we made it to Kenting. It's a nice little town by the beach, but I don't like the beach at all. So when we arrived, we relaxed for a bit and then headed to the beach, where I quickly realized that I really didn't care for it. So I rented a bicycle and did a 10 km ride down to the southernmost point of Taiwan. I started riding a little late, so I only had about 15 minutes there before I had to cycle those 10 km back to avoid riding in complete darkness.

Kenting is alright. The town itself is quiet, but at night there's a night market that feels like it was taken straight out of Thailand. Also, for some unexplained reason, I met so many Israelis there. Maybe this is where all the Israelis in Taiwan are.

The following day we went to Kenting National Park. We only arrived there at around 3 PM because Jacey and Hagan worked in the morning (they did a half day remotely). The hike itself wasn't that hard. It was more like a trail loop that started on the road and ended on the road farther down. We Ubered there, but we had to walk the 5 km back to town. It wasn't that bad, but I was hiking in sandals and my feet really hurt by the time we got back.

When we came back, we ate and walked around the night market. I did, however, learn through a random conversation with Jacey and Hagan that guava is basically king of the fruits. It's apperantly one of the healthiest fruits you can eat. I was absolutely amazed by it! I guess life can be a guava!

After that, we made our way to Chiayi, stayed a night there, and then headed to Alishan. Chiayi is not the best city in the world, but two years ago, when I was traveling, I went to a very nice hipster café and the owner even let me play his PS5 there. By chance, we ended up at the same place, so that was a nice throwback.

Alishan

When I first visited Taiwan during my big trip, I skipped this place. I remember saying I was "hiked out" from all the hikes I'd done (which weren't that many).

Anyway, on the first day we decided to stroll around Alishan National Park, where we'd pretty much have to go back every day since there wasn't much else to do in the area.

We were pretty exhausted on the first day because me and Hagan had encountered a really bad snorer in our hostel. So we didn't get much sleep, and it was also foggy.

We stayed at a tea farm around 10 km away from the national park. It was such a calm and quiet place, I loved it. And to my surprise, they had a piano!

Also, the guy who owns the place is absolutely the sweetest. He kept offering us rides whenever we needed them, to the supermarket, the bus stop, wherever. He even offered to take us to Alishan National Park, which is a 30 minute drive! We sent him pictures when we reached the summit of Yushan, and he was so proud of us.

The following day, we made our way to Alishan National Park again and hiked the Mianyue Trail. It was a short hike from the park itself to the trailhead. During the hike, a Taiwanese couple basically adopted us. The lady was super fond of us, and Jacey in particular because she speaks Chinese, but her husband kept trying to convince her to part ways with us! Oh well, it was fun. During the hike we had to go through three tunnels and climb over a section that had collapsed because of a typhoon last year.

The day after that, we woke up and decided to take a "chill" day, which ended up being not chill at all. Me and Jacey wanted to do a short trail from where we were staying to a coffee place that was supposed to be at the end of the hike. But we got lost and accidentally kept walking in circles around our accommodation. So we had coffee nearby, managed to hitchhike to a grocery store with an older couple, and then hitchhiked back. That's when we waited for Hagan and managed to find the trail we were looking for, which turned out to be the wrong one again :/

We ended up on a really weird trail that was technically closed because of that same typhoon. It was really run down and full of itchy plants and mosquitoes. Somehow we ended up on the opposite side of where we were staying. So we hitchhiked again to that same grocery store from the morning and made our way back.

Yushan

The day before the Yushan hike, which is the real reason we went on this trip, we stayed at a hostel next to the trailhead, as it's pretty much necessary if you're doing it without a car.

We stayed at Dongpu Lodge. It only has showers from 16:00–20:30, and lights out is at 20:30. They also made us watch a safety video beforehand. And oh bother, it was a full-on scare campaign. I was terrified by the end of it. But I messaged my flatmate, who had climbed it before, and he told me the trail is maintained really well and that I shouldn't be scared at all.

The place was alright. It had nothing there except bare-bones bathrooms and beds. I thought the beds were a little concentration camp style. They were basically a long wooden platform with curtains separating people. I felt boxed in. But it didn't really matter, we were there to get a bit of sleep and start the climb anyway.

The following morning, we started the hike at around 8:10. We took the shuttle to the trailhead and began. Obviously, the trail was really, really easy. You can't get lost, and it's basically just a steady incline. I genuinely think Hehuanshan was harder than most of this trail (at least until Paiyun Lodge).

We made it to Paiyun Lodge, where we'd spend the evening before starting the summit hike at 2:30 AM. Literally three minutes after we arrived, it started pouring. And this is where I got really nervous. The summit trail is alright. It wasn't that bad in hindsight, but before I did it, everyone told me it was really easy unless it was raining. So we kept debating whether we should turn back or not and decided we'd make the call in the morning.

The lodge itself was fantastic. The beds were more spacious, and by pure luck we got a room to ourselves. The place had also just been renovated, so everything was new. The dinner was fantastic too. Honestly, it was much better than Dongpu Lodge.

Anyway, we tried to get some sleep and woke up around 1 AM to get organized and start the summit push. It was still raining, but only drizzling, so we decided to go for it anyway (even though we were all pretty scared).

It was the first time I'd ever hiked in pitch black darkness, and oh bother was it scary. Most of the trail was straightforward, just like the first 8.5 km, but near the end it got pretty sketchy and I could definitely see how a slip could result in a broken bone.

But nonetheless, we reached the summit at 4:50 AM!

It was absolutely freezing at the top because of the strong winds, and we were soaked from the rain. But I have to say it was well worth it. I don't think I'll forget this experience anytime soon. And honestly, in hindsight, the rain wasn't that bad. You just have to be a bit more careful, and the views might be worse because everything is foggy. I will say, though, that if it had been pouring the whole time, I wouldn't recommend attempting the summit.

After that, we raced down the mountain because we had a bus to catch at 12:08 back to Alishan, and from there we went to Chiayi and then all the way to Taipei. The descent back to Paiyun Lodge took us an hour and twenty minutes because it was steep and we were stuck behind a large group moving very slowly. From Paiyun Lodge back down to the trailhead, we managed it in just three hours! We were very impressed with ourselves.

And that was that, 3952 meters up and then all the way back to Taipei in a single day. I absolutely loved that trip, although I was completely exhausted by the time we got back.

When we got to Taipei, we treated ourselves to a massive burger and then promptly passed out in our hostel until the next day. Then we just killed time until our flight. (Obviously, I went back to the pottery studio there that I love so much.)

And that was that! I don't think I'll be hiking anytime soon because I'm pretty hiked out. But I am considering doing a bicycle trip around Taiwan. We'll see.
